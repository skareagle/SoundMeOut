    How to run this, and step settings

    This step

    Moving on is your call. Repeating an easy step costs nothing.

    Ten minutes, and he does the reading aloud — not you. The stories are dull on purpose; the job here is decoding practice, and the real books come afterwards when you read to him.

    The routine

    1. Warm up on the word list until each one is quick.
    2. Read the "know by heart" words to him, twice.
    3. He reads the story aloud. If he stalls on a word for more than about three seconds, point at the first letter and say the sound — don't say the word.
    4. Read the same story again tomorrow and the day after. Tick a dot each time. The third pass is where fluency shows up, and where he hears himself sound like a reader.

    Marking stumbles

    Turn on "Mark stumbles" and tap the words he got stuck on, or long-press a word at any time. Press "Finish" at the end: the marked words are saved and come back in tomorrow's warm-up.

    What not to do

    • Don't say "what would make sense there?" or "look at the picture" — there are no pictures here for exactly that reason.
    • Don't move to the next step until the current one is comfortable. Repeating an easy step costs nothing; skipping ahead costs weeks.
    • Don't correct every slip mid-sentence. Let him finish the line, then go back to the one word.
